The idea

Steering is easy to show. Control is hard to prove.

Activation steering shows that patching internal states can move a frozen model. It leaves three questions open: which few sites to patch, how hard to push, and what evidence shows that the change behaves like control.

Sparse internal control answers them as a control problem. Selected activations become temporary, adjustable inputs, the weights never change, and success is defined by task readouts, an energy budget and a certificate checked in the full model.

  • Few sites, chosen on purpose

    Driver-OMP and Coverage-Driver select at most eight sites from a library of attention heads, MLP outputs and residual positions.

    How it works

  • A certificate for every intervention

    Target recovery, off-target change and energy are checked together in the original nonlinear model.

    See the certificate

  • Controllability you can compute

    Near the unpatched model, reaching a target reduces to a span test and a minimum energy, with a bound on linearization error.

    Explore the theory

  • Six axes instead of one score

    Reachability, reversal, dose response, feedback, preservation and reuse are measured separately, so one strong number cannot hide a failure.

    See the six axes

Framework

Specify, select, certify

The framework separates what you ask for from how drivers are chosen. Any selector can be plugged into the same specification and judged by the same certificate.

Two selectors, one pipeline

Driver-OMP fits the requested displacement by sparse residual fitting. Coverage-Driver adds candidates by saturated coverage across prompts.

Local proposalShared refitNonlinear validation

The sparse control problem

\(\displaystyle\min_{\beta \in \mathbb{R}^M}\ \|\beta\|_{0,c} + \lambda_E\, E(\beta) \quad \text{s.t. } \beta \text{ is an } (\varepsilon, \delta, \kappa) \text{ certificate}\)

Weighted sparsity \(\|\beta\|_{0,c} = \sum_{v \in S_\beta} c_v\) counts the selected sites; the energy \(E(\beta) = \beta^{\top} W \beta\) bounds how hard they are pushed.

Evaluation protocol

One push is a demo. Control means you can hit a target, undo it, dial it, track it, spare everything else, and reuse it on prompts you have never seen.

推动一次只是演示。控制意味着你能命中目标、撤销它、调节它、跟踪它、不扰动其余一切，并在从未见过的提示上复用它。

Every axis reads the same patched model \(f_\theta^{\gamma\beta}\) with the sites, strengths and directions fixed. Only the dose \(\gamma\) and the inputs change.

\(-\beta\) on the target input \(x^{c}\)

Does the opposite patch send a target input back toward its source?

\(\mathrm{Reversal}(\beta) = \dfrac{\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathrm{dis}\to\mathrm{clean}}}{\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathrm{dis}\to\mathrm{src}}}\)
Passes when

Dose response

ten doses \(\gamma \in \Gamma\) · target prompts

As the dose grows, does progress toward the target grow in order?

\(P(\gamma) = \dfrac{\langle q, F(\gamma\beta)\rangle}{\|q\|_2^2 + \xi},\ \ \rho = \mathrm{Spearman}(\gamma, P)\)
Passes when
\(\rho \ge 0.95\) over ten doses \(\gamma \in \Gamma = \{-1, \dots, 2\}\).
In the paper
All 48 Refusal curves have \(\rho = 1\), yet none passes signed reversal. Order and reversibility are different properties.

Feedback controllability

\(\gamma_t\) from feedback · target prompts

Keeping the sites fixed, can a feedback loop on the dose track new targets?

\(\gamma_{t+1} = \gamma_t + \eta\,\big(m^{\star} - M(\gamma_t)\big)\)
Passes when
\(|m^{\star} - M(\gamma_{\mathrm{end}})| \le \tau_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\): 0.3 for IOI and MCQA, 0.1 for Refusal.

Off-target preservation

\(\gamma = 1\) · off-target prompts

Does the patch leave unrelated prompts alone?

\(L_{\mathrm{off}}(\beta) = \tfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{off}}}\sum_j \mathrm{KL}\big(p_\theta(\cdot\,|\,\tilde x_j)\,\|\,p_\theta^{\beta}(\cdot\,|\,\tilde x_j)\big)\)
Passes when
\(\mathrm{KL} \le \delta = 0.001\), as a seed mean and on every prompt.
In the paper
No MCQA or Sentiment seed passes. On Refusal, Driver-OMP passes 4/8 seeds by the mean rule.

Held-out reuse

\(\gamma = 1\) · unseen prompts

Do the chosen sites, or the whole intervention, carry over to new prompts?

\(\mathrm{recovery} = \dfrac{\bar b_{\mathrm{patched}} - \bar b_{\mathrm{source}}}{\bar b_{\mathrm{target}} - \bar b_{\mathrm{source}}}\)
Passes when
No pass flag. Recovery is reported with its condition: refit \(\beta\) or frozen \(\beta\).
In the paper

Sites travel, frozen interventions do not

Held-out reuse stays a continuous number with its information condition attached, never a pass flag. In module level MCQA, Driver-OMP keeps its selected sites and refits strengths on held out targets to recover 0.992 ± 0.009 (Humanities) and 0.987 ± 0.034 (STEM). Freeze the whole intervention and recovery falls to 0.021 ± 0.026 and 0.024 ± 0.066.

Sentiment shows the opposite pattern at \(k = 8\): training mean directions give a mean margin recovery of 0.942 ± 0.535 for Coverage-Driver, yet no F seed passes signed reversal.

Theory

The mathematics of internal control

Near the unpatched model, a frozen Transformer acts as a linear map from intervention strengths to behavior. Choosing drivers then becomes a controllability question: can the selected actuators reach the requested displacement, and at what energy? The local analysis proposes a support and strengths. The nonlinear certificate decides whether to accept them.

In the span, but the cheapest exact fit needs more energy than κ.

The reachable set \(\{\bar G_S\beta : \beta^{\top}\beta \le \kappa\}\) is an ellipse, symmetric about the unpatched model. The orange point is the energy constrained best fit; the red dashes are the residual that \(\chi_\kappa(S)\) normalizes.

  • Definition · B.1

    Local effect dictionary

    \(g_{i,v} = J_{i,s_v}\, u_v(x_i)\)\(J_{i,s_v} = \dfrac{\partial b(f_\theta, x_i)}{\partial h_{s_v}(x_i)}\)\(q \approx G\beta\)

    Each candidate’s first order effect on every target readout is one column of \(G\), taken at the unpatched model. A preservation dictionary \(H\) is built the same way from a differentiable surrogate of the off-target observable.

  • Theorem 1

    Local linearization error

    \(\|\Phi_S(\beta_S) - G_S\beta_S\|_2 \le \dfrac{L_S}{2}\, \beta_S^{\top} W_{SS}\, \beta_S\)

    Inside the energy ball the model departs from its linearization by at most the curvature bound \(L_S\) times half the energy. Target recovery therefore holds whenever \(\|q - G_S\beta_S\|_2 + L_S\kappa/2 \le \varepsilon(\|q\|_2 + \xi)\).

  • Equation B.19

    Local target controllability

    \(\chi_\kappa(S) = \displaystyle\min_{\beta_S^{\top} W_{SS}\beta_S \le \kappa} \frac{\|\bar q - \bar G_S\beta_S\|_2}{\|\bar q\|_2 + \xi}\)

    A support reaches the target exactly at first order within budget if and only if two conditions hold: \(\bar q \in \operatorname{range}(\bar G_S)\), a span condition, and \(E_{\min}(S) \le \kappa\), an energy condition.

  • Theorem 2

    Minimum energy interpolation

    \(\beta_S^{\dagger} = W_{SS}^{-1/2}\, \tilde G_S^{+}\, \bar q\)\((\beta_S^{\dagger})^{\top} W_{SS}\, \beta_S^{\dagger} \le \dfrac{\|\bar q\|_2^2}{\sigma_{\min}^{+}(\tilde G_S)^2}\)

    In whitened coordinates \(\tilde G_S = \bar G_S W_{SS}^{-1/2}\), the cheapest exact fit is a pseudoinverse. A support with a small nonzero singular value can demand far more energy to reach the same target.

  • Proposition 1

    Energy constrained refit

    \(\beta_S^{\star}(\nu) = \big(\hat G_S^{\top}\hat G_S + (\lambda + \nu) W_{SS}\big)^{-1} \hat G_S^{\top}\hat q\)

    Target fit and a quadratic preservation term are refit jointly on the chosen support. Energy decreases monotonically in the multiplier \(\nu\), so the budget \(\kappa\) is met exactly by bisection.

  • Proposition 2

    Saturated coverage

    \(C(S) = \sum_j w_j \min\Big(1, \sum_{v \in S} a_{jv}\Big)\)\(C(S_{\mathrm{greedy}}) \ge (1 - 1/e)\, C(S^{\star})\)

    Coverage is monotone submodular, so greedy selection under a pure cardinality budget is within \(1 - 1/e\) of optimal. In its simplest setting Driver-OMP reduces to orthogonal matching pursuit.

Local results only propose. An intervention is accepted when the \((\varepsilon, \delta, \kappa)\) certificate holds in the original nonlinear model, and the six axes then characterize the control it provides.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does sparse internal control change the model’s weights?
No. The parameters \(\theta\) stay frozen. An intervention adds scaled directions to a few activations during the forward pass, and setting \(\beta = 0\) recovers the original model exactly.
What is a driver node?
A candidate control node is a triple of a physical site, an intervention direction and a selection cost. The candidates that receive a nonzero strength form the driver set \(S_\beta = \operatorname{supp}(\beta)\). One site can host several candidates with different directions.
How is this different from activation steering?
Activation steering shows that internal directions can change behavior. Sparse internal control adds the control problem around it: which compact set of sites to use, under a bounded energy, with explicit requirements for target recovery and off-target preservation, followed by an evaluation on six axes.
Why six axes instead of one score?
Because the properties separate in practice. Refusal responds to dose in perfect order and still fails reversal. MCQA sites transfer when strengths are refit, while the fully frozen intervention barely moves held out prompts. One number would hide both facts.
What do P and F mean?
They describe the direction information. P uses evaluation pair directions, computed from the activation contrast of each evaluation pair. F uses training mean directions, averaged over the fitting split and shared across inputs.
Which models and tasks were tested?
Indirect object identification and sentiment on GPT-2 small, MMLU Humanities and STEM on Qwen3-4B, and refusal on Qwen3-1.7B. The appendix adds model comparisons on Qwen3-8B and Mistral-7B.
Can I reproduce the tables without a GPU?
Yes. The record replay path recalculates the paper’s tables from the supplied measurements on CPU, and verify compares 162 numerical rows and 15 supporting values against the manuscript. Running the models themselves uses a separate environment and execution profile.
Can I evaluate my own steering method with the protocol?
Yes. Each axis is defined on any intervention with a support, base strengths and a direction rule. Directions from steering vectors, sparse autoencoder features or editing methods can enter as candidates and be scored on the same six axes.
